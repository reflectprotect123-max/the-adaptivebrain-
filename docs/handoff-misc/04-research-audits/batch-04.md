# The Everyday Responder research — Batch 04

## Scope and method

Assigned range: Episodes 26–33.

Metadata was cross-checked against the official Buzzsprout archive/RSS feed and each official Buzzsprout episode page. Spotify was used only as a distribution cross-check. No full transcripts are reproduced here.

All eight assigned pages expose official show notes only. No public transcript text or chapter timestamps were visible on the episode pages checked. The summaries below therefore reflect the published show notes and are deliberately conservative where the episode’s spoken answer is not publicly available.

## Episode 26

- **Title:** 5 Nutrition Tactics for Tactical Athletes Who Hate Meal Prep
- **Date:** 2025-05-09
- **Duration:** 32:57
- **Official URL:** https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/17132225-episode-26-5-nutrition-tactics-for-tactical-athletes-who-hate-meal-prep
- **Transcript status:** Show notes only; no public transcript or chapters visible.
- **Research summary:** The episode presents a practical nutrition system for athletes whose schedules make ideal meal preparation unrealistic. The five published tactics are to break meal prep into smaller tasks, fix the meals that repeatedly fail, maintain a nutrient-dense food rotation, keep reliable on-the-go options, and use meal-prep services when useful. A linked pre/post-training nutrition guide is also promoted.
- **Key notes:**
  - Nutrition adherence is framed as a systems problem rather than a search for perfect conditions.
  - The focus is on repeatable food availability and recovery support around training.
  - No detailed calorie, macronutrient, or timing prescription is publicly available from the show notes.
- **Tags:** nutrition, meal-prep, fueling, recovery, adherence
- **Hybrid relevance:** Useful as a simple Resources item supporting the two-strength/two-conditioning week. Keep any in-app application to a few repeatable fueling habits rather than adding a complex diet layer to the training workflow.

## Episode 28

- **Title:** 5 Questions That Separate Mission-Ready Athletes From Box Checkers
- **Date:** 2025-05-23
- **Duration:** 28:57
- **Official URL:** https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/17214089-episode-28-5-questions-that-separate-mission-ready-athletes-from-box-checkers
- **Transcript status:** Show notes only; no public transcript or chapters visible.
- **Research summary:** The episode offers a readiness filter built around five questions: whether the athlete can perform at 3 a.m.; whether training is moving toward a meaningful long-term vision; whether weaknesses are deliberately targeted; whether daily actions match future goals; and whether the current intensity can be sustained for years rather than months.

## Episode 30

- **Title:** Persistence > Perfection: Auto-Regulation for Tactical Athletes
- **Date:** 2025-06-13
- **Duration:** 31:44
- **Official URL:** https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/17331733-episode-30-persistence-perfection-auto-regulation-for-tactical-athletes
- **Transcript status:** Show notes only; no public transcript or chapters visible.
- **Research summary:** The episode outlines five ways to auto-regulate while continuing to make progress: manage the training schedule, create session tiers, understand session and exercise intent, adapt movement patterns, and adjust for the current season of life or work. The notes say the framework was used during a demanding drill week while still allowing PRs.
- **Key notes:**
  - Auto-regulation is presented as a way to preserve persistence, not as permission to abandon progression.
  - Session tiers give the athlete a way to scale the day while retaining the session’s purpose.
  - Movement substitutions should preserve the intended pattern or quality.
  - Current work/life demands are part of programming context.
- **Tags:** autoregulation, session-tiers, scheduling, exercise-intent, substitutions, progression, readiness
- **Hybrid relevance:** Excellent fit for the Hybrid workflow. Keep the main lift progression and accessory intent intact, then use a small number of predefined adjustments for time, fatigue, or equipment. Conditioning should retain its chosen modality while scaling duration or intensity.

## Episode 32

- **Date:** 2025-06-30
- **Duration:** 42:23
- **Official URL:** https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/17425470-episode-32-everyday-responder-pillar-1-prs-with-purpose

## Episode 33

- **Title:** Why Experienced Tactical Athletes Are Getting Outperformed
- **Date:** 2025-07-07
- **Duration:** 1:03:12
- **Official URL:** https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/17462761-episode-33-why-experienced-tactical-athletes-are-getting-outperformed
- **Transcript status:** Show notes only; no public transcript or chapters visible.
- **Research summary:** The episode addresses experienced athletes whose performance is declining despite their knowledge and history. The notes argue that experience becomes an advantage when the athlete stops training as they did in the past and builds a system for their current reality. The emphasis is on removing what is holding performance back and making the system work with current demands.
- **Key notes:**
  - Experience does not remove the need to reassess the current training system.
  - Decline is framed as something that can be addressed through a better-fitting system, not simply accepted as aging.
  - The public notes do not identify specific exercises, volumes, or recovery prescriptions.
- **Tags:** experience, longevity, readiness, self-assessment, sustainability, recovery
- **Hybrid relevance:** Relevant to future tiers or modifications: preserve the same simple progression while adjusting volume, exercise choice, or conditioning stress to the athlete’s current recovery and work demands. Avoid age-based assumptions.

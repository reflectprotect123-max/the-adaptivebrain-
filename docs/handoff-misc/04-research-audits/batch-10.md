## Episode 74

- **Title:** My Training Has Never Been Better. Here’s the System — And What I’d Steal if I Were You.
- **Date:** 24 April 2026
- **Duration:** 51:28
- **Official URL:** [Buzzsprout episode 74](https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/19071000-74-my-training-has-never-been-better-here-s-the-system-and-what-i-d-steal-if-i-were-you)
- **Transcript status:** Public transcript visible. The Buzzsprout page exposes the transcript body with timestamped speaker content.
- **Summary:** Uses the host’s current busy season as a case study for building a training system around real stress, fatigue, schedule changes, nutrition, sleep, and recovery. The emphasis is on extracting principles and methods rather than copying one person’s exact split. The system values enough training stimulus to progress while preserving energy for work and the next day.
- **Key notes:** Use uncomplicated compound movement patterns; let the current season determine the amount of work; keep sessions time-aware; maintain easy activity and recovery work around training; protect sleep and nutrition; do not confuse soreness or exhaustion with a successful session.
- **Useful timestamps:** The transcript begins at `0:00`; the page’s transcript panel provides the remaining speaker timecodes.
- **Tags:** `system-design` `autoregulation` `compound-lifts` `recovery` `sleep` `nutrition` `time-efficient`
- **Hybrid relevance:** Strong support for a simple four-session week. Keep the program’s main work fixed and legible, then use small, controlled adjustments for life stress. Do not add a second “busy season” program on top of the existing 2-strength/2-conditioning structure.

## Episode 76

- **Title:** Easy Runs Don’t Build Weak Tactical Athletes
- **Date:** 11 May 2026
- **Duration:** 31:54
- **Official URL:** [Buzzsprout episode 76](https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/19161015-76-easy-runs-don-t-build-weak-tactical-athletes)
- **Transcript status:** Public transcript visible. The Buzzsprout page exposes the transcript body with timestamped speaker content.
- **Summary:** Defends easy running as foundational work rather than filler. It addresses common errors such as turning every run into a test, using arbitrary watch targets as the only authority, skipping easy base work, and failing to review the session afterward. Easy work should support recovery, running economy, and readiness for harder work.
- **Key notes:** Use conversational effort and controlled breathing as practical guides; walking can be an intentional way to stay easy; do not let pride dictate the pace; record objective data but review it after the run; use an after-action review to compare effort, pace, heart rate, recovery, and context; easy running should coexist with faster running rather than replace it.

- **Tags:** `easy-aerobic` `running-base` `RPE` `autoregulation` `recovery` `after-action-review` `readiness`
- **Hybrid relevance:** Directly supports the planned easy conditioning day. Define the modality first, then give a simple effort target and duration progression. Keep the easy day genuinely easy so the moderate day has a distinct purpose.

## Episode 82

- **Title:** How Tactical Athletes PR the 2 Mile (Part 1 — The Running)
- **Date:** 26 June 2026
- **Duration:** 41:25
- **Official URL:** [Buzzsprout episode 82](https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/19402644-82-how-tactical-athletes-pr-the-2-mile-part-1-the-running)
- **Transcript status:** Public transcript visible. The Buzzsprout page exposes the transcript body with timestamped speaker content.
- **Summary:** Part one of a four-part 2-mile series. It teaches RPE as a skill and organizes running into easy, moderate, and hard efforts. Easy work is conversational and sustainable; moderate work is controlled but demanding; hard repeats are uncomfortable but paced; maximal testing is kept separate from ordinary training. The episode also stresses reviewing data after the session rather than chasing a watch during it.
- **Key notes:** RPE 4–6 = easy/conversational; RPE 6–8 = moderate/controlled urgency; RPE 8.5–9 = hard repeats; RPE 10 = testing; practice pacing and aim for controlled or negative splits; record pace and heart-rate data but do not let them override the day’s effort target; use running, bike, rower, incline, or sled options when appropriate; do not make every conditioning session zone two or a time trial.
- **Useful timestamps:** The transcript opens at `0:00` with the RPE framework; the page’s public transcript panel provides the timecodes for the three run categories and data-review discussion.
- **Tags:** `running` `RPE` `easy-aerobic` `moderate-conditioning` `hard-intervals` `pacing` `testing` `autoregulation`
- **Hybrid relevance:** This is the clearest model for the planned conditioning workflow. Select the modality first, then prescribe one effort category and progress duration, intervals, or density across the block. Keep the easy and moderate days distinct; do not collapse them into a generic circuit.

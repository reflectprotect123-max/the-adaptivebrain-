## Episode 18

- **Title:** Go/No-Go’s That Aren’t Standards but Should Be
- **Date:** March 15, 2025
- **Duration:** 24:27
- **Official page:** [Episode 18](https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/16796199-episode-18-go-no-go-s-that-aren-t-standards-but-should-be)
- **Transcript status:** Show notes only; no public transcript visible.
- **Research summary:** The episode frames readiness around standards that may not be formal tests but still affect operational ability. Published notes identify relative strength/bodyweight handling, favorable body composition, deliberate “gut checks,” and non-negotiables outside the gym as the main areas.
- **Key notes:**
  - Readiness is broader than passing formal PT or appearance requirements.
  - Relative strength and body composition are treated as practical readiness markers.
  - A small set of explicit personal standards may reduce decision fatigue.
- **Tags:** `readiness`, `relative-strength`, `body-composition`, `standards`, `habits`, `tactical-athlete`
- **Hybrid relevance:** Could inform a short readiness checklist alongside the existing four-session week. Keep it to a few actionable markers; do not turn it into a large testing system.

## Episode 19

- **Title:** The Everyday Responder System; Step by Step Breakdown to Sustainable Results
- **Date:** March 21, 2025
- **Duration:** 31:55
- **Official page:** [Episode 19](https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/16837310-episode-19-the-everyday-responder-system-step-by-step-breakdown-to-sustainable-results)
- **Transcript status:** Show notes only; no public transcript visible.
- **Research summary:** The published notes describe a repeatable system beginning with goals and the user’s “why,” followed by movement assessments and benchmarks, core values, and an integrated view of training, nutrition, and lifestyle. The stated aim is sustainable target-hitting rather than repeated program changes or information overload.
- **Key notes:**
  - Start with a defined outcome and reason for training.
  - Use assessments and benchmarks to establish direction.
  - Treat training, nutrition, and lifestyle as connected inputs.
  - A system should reduce program hopping and analysis paralysis.
- **Tags:** `systems`, `goal-setting`, `assessment`, `benchmarks`, `lifestyle`, `consistency`, `sustainability`
- **Hybrid relevance:** Strong fit for a simple onboarding workflow: goal → baseline → four-week block → review. This supports KISS if the app exposes only the decisions needed to start the next block.

- **Date:** April 12, 2025

- **Official page:** [Episode 22](https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/16966997-episode-22-program-hopping-is-killing-your-progress-here-s-the-smarter-approach-for-tactical-athletes)
- **Transcript status:** Show notes only; no public transcript visible.
- **Research summary:** The notes argue that frequent program changes interfere with consistent, measurable progress. The alternative described is to make controlled programming adjustments inside a coherent system, using novelty only when it serves the larger plan.
- **Key notes:**
  - Keep the main structure stable long enough to evaluate it.
  - Make targeted tweaks rather than replacing the whole plan.

## Episode 24

- **Title:** Q & A - Mental toughness and grit, building up runs after a hiatus, diet “kick starts”, training around shift work, step by step to navigating injuries, & more
- **Date:** April 29, 2025
- **Duration:** 45:18
- **Official page:** [Episode 24](https://theeverydayresponder.buzzsprout.com/2427372/episodes/17067154-episode-24-q-a-mental-toughness-and-grit-building-up-runs-after-a-hiatus-diet-kick-starts-training-around-shift-work-step-by-step-to-navigating-injuries-more)
- **Transcript status:** Show notes only; no public transcript visible.
- **Research summary:** The Q&A covers diet “kick starts,” training around shift work, injury navigation, mental toughness versus grit, performance changes during weight loss, and rebuilding running after time away. These topics point to a need for adaptable entry points and sensible return-to-training decisions.
- **Key notes:**

  - Weight-loss phases can change performance and should be interpreted in context.
- **Tags:** `shift-work`, `return-to-running`, `injury-management`, `weight-loss`, `mental-toughness`, `adaptation`, `q-and-a`
- **Hybrid relevance:** Strong case for letting users move the four weekly sessions across available days while keeping strength and conditioning separate. Any injury flow should be conservative and direct users to qualified clinical advice where appropriate.

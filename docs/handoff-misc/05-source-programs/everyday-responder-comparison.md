## Publicly confirmed architecture

- Paid program name: Uniform Strong.
- Duration: 16 weeks.
- Three phases: Build the Engine → Skill Acquisition → Testing.
- Assessments: Weeks 0, 9, and 16.
- Modality options: run, row, or bike.
- Uses RPE/RIR autoregulation.
- Advertised commitment: five training days completed within a flexible D1–D7 window.
- Includes an eight-week running base builder and an exercise-substitution guide.

## Public example week

- Day 1: plyometrics, four full-body compound lifts, anaerobic bike finisher; 45–60 minutes.
- Day 2: 45 minutes low-intensity continuous run/ruck/row/bike.
- Day 3: off/flex.
- Day 4: full-body strength plus grip/carries/circuit work; approximately 45 minutes.
- Day 5: moderate tempo intervals around 80–85%; approximately 30 minutes.
- Days 6–7: off/flex.

This published example totals roughly 3.5–4 hours and may describe coaching methodology rather than every exact Uniform Strong week.

## Comparison with THE HYBRID ENGINE

| Feature | Everyday Responder | THE HYBRID ENGINE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main goal | Tactical readiness | Strength, muscle, conditioning, longevity |
| Normal structure | Five training days advertised | Two strength + two conditioning |
| Minimum week | Not publicly specified | Two strength sessions |
| Strength | Two full-body days in public example | Two full-body days |
| Conditioning | Easy aerobic, tempo, and anaerobic exposure | Easy aerobic plus progressive intervals |
| Scheduling | Flexible D1–D7 | Flexible sequence; missed work rolls forward |
| Autoregulation | RPE/RIR and readiness | RIR plus Ready/Managed/Not-ready lanes |
| Testing | Formal Weeks 0/9/16 | Testing recognised as a gap |
| Work adjustment | General shift-work flexibility | Explicit Christmas delivery maintenance phase |

## Honest assessment retained

- Everyday Responder as a general tactical system: 8/10.
- Direct copy for this user during aerobic re-entry and a physical delivery job: 6.5/10.
- Current customised Hybrid Engine: 8.5/10.
- Hybrid Engine after formal testing and a small power slot: approximately 9/10.

Best features to borrow:

- formal baselines and retesting;
- an explicit primary/secondary/tertiary quality hierarchy;
- a small plyometric/power primer;
- flexible sequence-based scheduling.

Features not copied blindly:

- five formal training days;
- anaerobic finishers after strength during high work fatigue;
- maximal testing during the late-November-to-Christmas delivery peak;
- tactical standards that do not match the user's actual job or goals.
